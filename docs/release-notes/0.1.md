# GridForge 0.1

**DRAFT for David (2026-11-18). Not published; the tag and the release
page are David's to make.**

GridForge is a meshing and simulation workbench for Linux: a part's
geometry in, meshed, set up, solved, and its results out to ParaView, each
step said in the window's banner. 0.1 solves static structural studies
with CalculiX.

## Getting it

- **AppImage** (`GridForge-0.1-x86_64.AppImage`): one file, CalculiX's
  solver (ccx 2.23) inside. Make it executable and run it.
- **Ubuntu 26.04 and newer**: `gridforge` and `gridforge-calculix` (.deb);
  `sudo apt install ./gridforge_*.deb ./gridforge-calculix_*.deb`.
- **From FreeCAD**: the macro `GridForge.FCMacro` (tools/freecad) names
  groups of faces and exports the part as `.brep` with its groups.

Beside them on this page: the source of the ccx the AppImage carries
(`ccx_2.23.src.tar.bz2`, `spooles.2.2.tgz`) and the source package.

## What it does

- **Geometry**: STEP (every body in the file) or FreeCAD's `.brep` with
  its named groups; touching solids joined so they share their nodes.
- **Meshing** (Netgen, from GridForge's fork): tetrahedra, linear or
  quadratic; two elements through a thin wall by default; every setting
  and the OpenCASCADE version written with the mesh; its quality measured
  and said.
- **Set-up in the window**: pick faces in the view; supports (fixed, per
  direction, symmetry) and loads (move, force, pressure) with their
  direction from the model (a face's normal, an axis, a picked edge) or by
  components; materials from a FreeCAD card or typed in, plasticity from a
  TenSense curve; several steps. Each drawn on the model.
- **Groups that notice**: a group remembers its faces' kind, area and
  centre, and says so when the geometry changes under it.
- **Solving** never freezes the window; every run is kept in its own
  folder with its deck, log and results.
- **Results**: coloured by von Mises stress (or plastic strain), the
  deformed shape with its factor said, increment by increment; Open in
  ParaView hands over one `.vtkhdf` with the named sets as blocks.
- **gf-study** does the same from the command line.

## How far the results were checked

- The test suite's boxes, solved by ccx 2.23, against hand answers:
  tension, pressure, gravity, force, two steps, plasticity.
- A dogbone bent as a cantilever: the tip within 0.10 % of beam theory
  (more flexible, as shear allows), and a moved tip's reaction equal to
  the push's stiffness (examples/dogbone-bend).
- The release walkthrough: from FreeCAD's export to a ParaView file by
  the banner alone; the study it made, solved by the real ccx 2.23,
  balances its load.

## Known limits of 0.1

- Static structural studies only (CalculiX); no contact yet; OpenFOAM
  later.
- Linux x86_64; the .deb needs Ubuntu 26.04 or newer (the first with a
  Qt 6 VTK). On Wayland it runs through XWayland.
- A mesh may differ by a few elements from one computer to another; on
  one computer the same study gives the same mesh.
- Two elements through thin walls costs elements (a 4 mm dogbone at 3 mm:
  24108 tetrahedra, not 1624); `throughWall` 1 or 0 in the mesh recipe
  for less.
- The FreeCAD macro's panel is checked by hand only; the files it writes
  are tested in FreeCAD's command line program.
- The application's mark is a placeholder.
